3.1  "If the end goal is pick and place, can't we just write a node for that?"

Yes, and that node is the right end product - but it is the TOP layer and
needs the layers under it:

  piece                     why the node needs it            status then
  ------------------------  -------------------------------  ------------
  forward kinematics        check where the arm ended up     done
  inverse kinematics        "pick at (x,y,z)" -> joint angles MISSING
  motion between poses      else the arm jumps A -> B         missing
  a way to command the arm  /joint_states for RViz;           RViz only
                            ros2_control or Quanser API later
  gripper open/close        cannot pick anything without it   not modelled
  object locations          hard-coded first, camera later    not started

THE 4-DOF DESIGN CONSTRAINT, worth remembering before designing any grasp:
with four joints you CANNOT choose the gripper's approach direction freely.
The first three joints spend their freedom reaching the position; the forearm
direction then follows from shoulder + elbow, and the wrist only rolls ABOUT
that direction. So a straight-down grasp is possible only at some positions;
elsewhere the gripper arrives at whatever angle the reach requires. This
affects where objects may be placed and how they must be grabbed.

The one application that does NOT need IK is teach-and-repeat: move the arm by
hand, record joint angles, play them back. Everything else (pick and place,
drawing, camera tracking, Cartesian teleop, path following) needs IK.

3.2  "Is forward_kinematics.py pretty much boilerplate?"

Mostly yes, and it is worth knowing which part is not.

  TEXTBOOK, identical for any arm:
    dh_transform()      the standard DH matrix, printed in every textbook
    link_transforms()   multiply the four row matrices in order
    forward_kinematics()
    position(), rpy_from_transform()
    validate_joint_vector(), format_pose(), main()

  SPECIFIC TO THE QARM - the four rows of dh_table(), i.e. 16 numbers:
    (q1,                 LAMBDA_1, 0.0,      -pi/2)
    (q2 + BETA - pi/2,   0.0,      LAMBDA_2,  0.0)
    (q3 + pi - BETA,     0.0,      0.0,      +pi/2)
    (q4,                 LAMBDA_3, 0.0,       0.0)

  That is where the engineering is: +BETA appears in row 2 ONLY and once;
  a_2 is the hypotenuse lambda_2, not L2; lambda_3 spans across joint 4; the
  +/-pi/2 terms align DH frames to the URDF axes. The Day 2 bug lived here.

  For interviews: be able to write dh_transform() from memory, and explain why
  each of those 16 entries has the value it does. Section 5 of
  docs/kinematics.md is the written version of that answer.

4.4  A FALSE CLAIM IN OUR OWN DOCS, corrected

Day 2's notes said: "symmetric ranges transfer no matter where zero sits."
THAT IS WRONG. If a zero is offset, a symmetric range becomes asymmetric in
the other convention. Example: if Quanser's shoulder zero were 8.13 deg from
ours, +/-85 deg would become -76.87 .. +93.13 deg in our angles.

The claim only holds when the zeros COINCIDE. They happen to coincide for
base, shoulder and wrist, and not for the elbow - so the conclusion survived,
but the reasoning behind it did not. Worth remembering: a conclusion that
happens to be right for the wrong reason is still a defect.

4.5  Kink direction  (OPEN-1) - CLOSED

Why no test can catch this: at the straight-arm pose the upper arm leans
8.13 deg to one side. A MIRROR-IMAGE model (leaning the other way) has
identical lengths, identical reach, and identical code-vs-URDF agreement. The
tests check exactly those things, and the code and URDF were written with the
same assumption, so they agree even if the assumption is wrong. Only the
drawing or the physical arm can tell them apart.

What it would have cost: commanding "tip the shoulder 30 deg forward" assumes
the arm already leans 8 deg forward, expecting 38 deg from vertical. A
mirrored arm would reach 22 deg - a 16 deg error at the shoulder, which with
~75 cm of arm past it moves the gripper about 21 cm. Pick and place would miss
every time.

Resolved two ways: Figure 3 shows the elbow offset on the side the arm reaches
toward, and the student confirmed a positive shoulder angle tips the arm
further the way it already leans. Both match the model. Still on the hardware
checklist as a 30-second confirmation.

5.1  WHY THE NEW -beta IN ROW 3 IS NOT THE DAY 2 DOUBLE-COUNT BUG

This is the single most important thing to be able to defend, because it looks
exactly like the bug that was fixed on Day 2.

  The Day 2 bug applied the kink's LENGTH twice - an L3 translation AND a beta
  pre-tilt - which shortened the effective upper arm from 353.6 mm to 346.5 mm.

  Here NO LENGTH IS TOUCHED. a_2 is still lambda_2 = 0.353553, and row 3 has
  d = 0 and a = 0. The -beta only says WHERE THE ELBOW'S ZERO POINTS: row 2
  aims along lambda_2 (tilted by +beta), but the elbow measures its angle from
  L2 (not tilted), so row 3 rotates back by beta to compensate.

  The length guard is test_kink_applied_exactly_once, which asserts the
  shoulder-to-elbow distance is the hypotenuse 0.353553 - ruling out both the
  double-applied 0.3465 and the omitted-kink 0.350 at the same time. It still
  passes.

================================================================================
        6. SESSION 1 - THE RPY BUG THE CHANGE EXPOSED
================================================================================

The new home pose points the tool horizontally, which is pitch = 90 deg
EXACTLY - RPY gimbal lock, where roll and yaw stop being separable and only
their sum is defined.

rpy_from_transform() computed cos(pitch) as sqrt(1 - sin^2(pitch)). At
pitch = 90 deg, 1e-16 of rounding in sin becomes 1e-8 in cos, which SAILED
PAST the gimbal-lock test (cos_pitch < 1e-9) and took the normal branch. The
home pose was reported as (180, 90, 180) deg - the same orientation as
(0, 90, 0), but confusing to read.

Fixed by reading cos(pitch) straight out of the matrix:

    cos_pitch = hypot(R[0,0], R[1,0])
    pitch     = atan2(-R[2,0], cos_pitch)

Now reports (0, 90, 0). The Day 2 code review had flagged this branch as
untested; the new zero-pose test exercises it, so it is now covered.

LESSON: sqrt(1 - x^2) loses half the available precision near |x| = 1. When
both components are available, use atan2/hypot on them directly.

display.launch.py lives in qarm_description. If it started a node from

(Later, qarm_move_to went the other way: it lives in qarm_kinematics with its

7.6  A lint rule that differs between the two packages - do not be confused
     by this later

The first test run failed:

  ./scripts/qarm_gripper.py:2 at module level: D213: Multi-line docstring
  summary should start at the second line

But every file in qarm_kinematics puts its summary on the FIRST line and

ASSUMPTION STILL UNPROVEN, flagged at the time: "clockwise" is taken as seen
from BEHIND the wrist looking out toward the fingertips. One constant,
WRIST_CLOCKWISE_SIGN in inverse_kinematics.py, flips it if the hardware
disagrees. Consequence of the +/-160 deg wrist limit: 0-160 deg turns
clockwise, 200-360 deg turns counter-clockwise the short way round
(270 clockwise == 90 counter-clockwise), and 161-199 deg is unreachable.

THE SUBTLE PART, AND THE REASON THIS IS WORTH A SECTION:

It reports that it successfully RAN the tests, not that they passed.

       10. SESSION 2 - OPEN-3, THE GUARD READ THE WRONG FILE
================================================================================

build_urdf_string() in validate_fk_vs_tf.py resolved the Xacro from the
INSTALLED share directory first, falling back to the source tree.

Why that is a real defect, not a nitpick: a plain `colcon build` COPIES the
Xacro into install/ (qarm_description is ament_cmake installing files, so no
symlink). Editing src/.../qarm.urdf.xacro and running the tests WITHOUT
rebuilding compared FK against the PREVIOUS model. The test would pass while
the model and the code disagreed - which is precisely the drift the test
exists to catch. The guard had a hole shaped like its own purpose.

Fixed: new xacro_path() tries, in order,
  1. next to this module in the source tree,
  2. <workspace>/src/qarm_description/... located from the install prefix
     (the standard colcon layout),
  3. the installed share directory, as a last resort when there is no source.

The comparison table now PRINTS which file it used ("urdf: ..."), so this can
never be ambiguous again.

The same L2 0.35 -> 0.36 edit is the proof: before the fix that edit was
invisible to the test; now it fails the suite.

11.1  THE SPLIT, and why it is legitimate

q4 rolls about an axis running straight down the forearm, and end_effector
sits ON that axis, so q4 cannot change the end-effector POSITION. This is
already asserted on both sides (FK: test_q4_does_not_move_the_end_effector;
TF tree: test_q4_does_not_change_position_in_tf). Therefore position depends
on q1, q2, q3 only, and the wrist angle passes straight through. The manual
states the same thing in section B.

11.2  STEP 1 - the base angle

The shoulder and elbow both pitch about horizontal axes, so the whole arm
always lies in ONE vertical plane through the base axis. The base turns that
plane to face the target:

    q1 = atan2(y, x)
    r  = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)      distance from the base axis, in the plane
    h  = z - lambda_1         height above the shoulder axis

and the rest of the problem is two-dimensional.

11.3  STEP 2 - a two-link arm in the plane

Measure directions as angles FROM VERTICAL, positive toward +r:

    phi_2 = direction of the upper arm = q2 + beta
    gamma = elbow bend away from straight  (0 = arm dead straight)

    r = lambda_2 sin(phi_2) + lambda_3 sin(phi_2 + gamma)
    h = lambda_2 cos(phi_2) + lambda_3 cos(phi_2 + gamma)

The shoulder-to-target distance D depends on the BEND ALONE - law of cosines
on the shoulder-elbow-target triangle:

    D^2 = lambda_2^2 + lambda_3^2 + 2 lambda_2 lambda_3 cos(gamma)
    cos(gamma) = (D^2 - lambda_2^2 - lambda_3^2) / (2 lambda_2 lambda_3)

so gamma = +acos(...) or -acos(...): the elbow can sit on either side of the
shoulder-target line. If |cos(gamma)| > 1 no triangle exists, which is exactly
the too-far / too-close test.

With gamma known, the upper arm points "at the target, minus the angle the
bent forearm contributes":

    phi_2 = atan2(r, h)
            - atan2(lambda_3 sin gamma, lambda_2 + lambda_3 cos gamma)

11.4  STEP 3 - back to joint angles

    q2 = phi_2 - beta               undo the kink                 (DH row 2)
    q3 = gamma + ELBOW_STRAIGHT     bend-from-straight -> joint angle, because
                                    the elbow's zero is the home pose and the
                                    arm is straight at ELBOW_STRAIGHT (row 3)

Note how directly the DH table pays off here: both conversions are one term
each, taken straight from rows 2 and 3.

11.5  CHOOSING ONE SOLUTION, and a detail that is easy to get wrong

solve() keeps the in-limit candidates and returns the shortest move from the
current pose. "Shortest" is the smallest MAX |dq| over the four joints -
NOT the sum.

Why: all four joints share one speed limit (pi/2 rad/s, Table 7, now
MAX_JOINT_SPEED in robot_params.py), so the move takes as long as the joint
with the furthest to go. Minimising the sum would prefer a move that shuffles
all four joints a medium amount over one that moves a single joint slightly
further, even though the second finishes sooner.

Ties within 1e-9 rad go to elbow up (keeps the forearm further from the
table), then to the facing family.

11.6  THE ONE SINGULARITY

x = y = 0 puts the target on the base axis. atan2(0, 0) has no answer and
every base angle reaches the point, because the arm just spins about it. The
solver keeps the CURRENT base angle there, so the base does not rotate for no
reason. This is the solver's only position singularity.

11.7  UNREACHABLE TARGETS - never a silent NaN

  TargetTooFarError     D > lambda_2 + lambda_3 = 0.753553 m
  TargetTooCloseError   D < |lambda_3 - lambda_2| = 0.046447 m  (dead zone)
  JointLimitError       geometry fine, every candidate breaks a limit;
                        carries .candidates and .violations so the caller can
                        see WHICH joints and by how much

All three subclass IKError, itself a ValueError, so a caller can catch one
specific case or all of them. A silent NaN would propagate into joint commands
and move the real arm somewhere unpredictable.

The inner dead zone is the small sphere a two-link arm cannot fold into.
Figure 5a of the manual shows the same hole, larger there because the manual's
drawing also accounts for the joint limits that this solver reports separately.

NOT handled: collisions with the worksurface, the base, or the arm itself.
There is no collision model yet (goal 4), so a returned solution can still be
a pose you would not want to command on hardware.


================================================================================
       12. SESSION 2 - FOUR CANDIDATES, TWO ARM SHAPES
================================================================================

This is the most interesting piece of geometry in the solver, and the one
worth being able to explain.

The base can also turn the OTHER way, q1 + 180 deg, with the arm leaning back
over the shoulder; in the plane that is r = -sqrt(x^2 + y^2). Two families
times two bends gives FOUR candidates.

They are NOT four shapes. Because the shoulder sits ON the base axis,

    atan2(-r, h) = -atan2(r, h)

so the backward solution with bend -gamma has phi_2' = -phi_2, and therefore:

    elbow height = lambda_1 + lambda_2 cos(phi_2') = same as facing(+gamma)
    elbow r-offset in WORLD coordinates             = also the same, because
      the plane's r-axis flipped AND the planar offset negated

Same world elbow position, same tool position, whole arm geometrically
identical. So:

    facing(+gamma)  ==  backward(-gamma)      one physical arm shape
    facing(-gamma)  ==  backward(+gamma)      the other shape

Four joint vectors, two shapes, each WRITTEN TWO WAYS.

Which writing is LEGAL is decided by the joint limits, and usually only one
is. The elbow reaches only 13.13 deg past straight in the negative direction
(q3 >= -95 deg, and straight is -81.87 deg), so the facing family is nearly
always elbow-up only. THEREFORE THE BACKWARD FAMILY IS HOW THE ARM REACHES ITS
SECOND SHAPE AT ALL. It is half the workspace, not a curiosity - which is why
answering "yes, both" to decision 1 mattered.

Worked example, target (0.3, 0.2, 0.5) m, from `qarm_ik`:

  reach      elbow     q1..q4 [deg]                         limits
  facing     up          33.690  -14.521   13.282    0.000  ok
  facing     down        33.690   88.349 -177.022    0.000  out: joint_2, joint_3
  backward   down      -146.310 -104.609   13.282    0.000  out: joint_2
  backward   up        -146.310   -1.740 -177.022    0.000  out: joint_3

Read it as two shapes: {facing up, backward down} is one shape, {facing down,
backward up} the other - note q3 = 13.282 in the first pair and -177.022 in
the second. Here only one writing of one shape survives the limits.

12.1  LABELLING up/down - a first attempt that was wrong

The obvious label is the sign of gamma. That is MEANINGLESS across the two
families, because the planar r-axis flips, so the same gamma sign puts the
elbow on opposite sides. Labelling by gamma would call one shape "up" when
reached facing and "down" when reached backward.

Settled on the ELBOW'S WORLD HEIGHT, which is physical and therefore
identical for the two writings of one shape - so the labels stay consistent no
matter which family a solution came from. It also matches the reason the
student preferred elbow up: keeping the forearm further from the table.


================================================================================
       13. SESSION 2 - NUMERICAL TRAPS IN THE IK
================================================================================

13.1  acos is vertical at 1

First version tested "is the arm straight?" with `gamma > EPS` after computing
gamma = acos(cos_gamma). At full reach, 1e-16 of rounding in cos_gamma becomes
about 1e-8 rad in gamma, because acos has infinite slope at its endpoint.
Result: the single straight-arm solution split into two spurious bent ones and
the exact-max-reach check failed.

Fixed by deciding on cos_gamma instead:

    if cos_gamma >= 1.0 - 1e-12:  -> treat as straight, one solution

1 - cos(gamma) < 1e-12 corresponds to gamma < ~1.4e-6 rad, which moves the tip
by under 1e-12 m. Documented in the function.

GENERAL LESSON, same family as the RPY bug in section 6: test the quantity you
actually have, before passing it through a function that amplifies its error.

13.2  The straight-elbow singularity is real, not a bug

Near full stretch the tool position barely depends on the bend, so joint
angles are not uniquely recoverable there even though positions are exact.
The random round-trip check therefore SKIPS poses within 1 degree of straight
(16 of 1000) and says so. That is a property of the arm, not of the code, and
it is worth knowing before trusting IK output near the workspace boundary.

13.3  Floating point: a + (b - a) is not always b

Found in the move_to node (section 16), by inspection rather than by test.
Details in section 17, challenge 8.

================================================================================
       15. SESSION 2 - THE REACH FINDING
                (most interesting result of the day)
================================================================================

Day 2 concluded that Section B's reach (0.800 / 0.940 m) is measured to the
FINGERTIPS, 46.4 mm past end_effector. Its argument was: the shortfall against
our chain is IDENTICAL in both directions, and a wrong link length would
project differently into horizontal and vertical, so it can only be a constant
length appended at the tip. L_gripper = 0.046447 and the gripper_tip frame
came from that.

Checking the arithmetic EXACTLY today:

    lambda_2            = 0.3535533906
    lambda_3            = 0.4000000000
    lambda_2 + lambda_3 = 0.7535533906   <- Section D 0.7536  (our model)
    2 * lambda_3        = 0.8000000000   <- Section B 0.800   EXACTLY
    L1 + 2 * lambda_3   = 0.9400000000   <- Section B 0.940   EXACTLY
    lambda_3 - lambda_2 = 0.0464466094
    0.800 - (l2 + l3)   = 0.0464466094   <- the "46.4 mm", identical
    0.940 - (L1+l2+l3)  = 0.0464466094   <- same again

Section B's numbers are not approximately anything. They are exactly 2*lambda_3
and L1 + 2*lambda_3, and the residual is exactly lambda_3 - lambda_2 to seven
decimals.

So there are TWO hypotheses, not one:

  H1 (Day 2)  Section B = lambda_2 + lambda_3 + L_gripper, to the fingertips.
              Requires the real gripper to measure 46.4466 mm.
  H2 (new)    Section B = lambda_3 + lambda_3, i.e. lambda_2 mistakenly
              replaced by lambda_3 somewhere in Quanser's own arithmetic.
              This FORCES the residual to be exactly lambda_3 - lambda_2,
              which is what we observe.

**BOTH HYPOTHESES PREDICT THE SAME SHORTFALL IN BOTH DIRECTIONS**, because
both reach figures are built from the same lambda sum:
    horizontal shortfall = 2*l3 - (l2 + l3)      = l3 - l2
    vertical shortfall   = L1 + 2*l3 - (L1+l2+l3) = l3 - l2
So DAY 2'S ARGUMENT CANNOT TELL THEM APART. It was presented as proof and it
is not. What it DOES still rule out is a wrong L1..L5 - and Section D confirms
those directly anyway, which is the stronger evidence.

H2 additionally explains why the residual lands on lambda_3 - lambda_2 to
seven decimals. Under H1 that is a coincidence.

WHAT CHANGED IN THE CODE: nothing. end_effector is untouched and still the
FK/IK target. L_gripper and gripper_tip are kept so Section B stays
reproducible. But L_gripper is now documented as UNVERIFIED rather than as a
measured dimension, and the reasoning in docs/kinematics.md section 7 is
rewritten to present both hypotheses honestly.

ONE MEASUREMENT SETTLES IT on hardware - palm face (end of L5) to fingertips:
    ~46.4 mm            -> H1 holds, L_gripper is real, keep it.
    clearly different   -> H2, Section B is an arithmetic slip. Set L_gripper
                           to the MEASURED value (gripper_tip then stops
                           reproducing Section B, which is the CORRECT
                           outcome) and treat Section D as the only
                           authoritative reach figure.

A COINCIDENCE NOT TO TRIP OVER: the IK inner dead-zone radius is
|lambda_3 - lambda_2| = 0.046447 m, numerically the same as L_gripper. They
are unrelated quantities that happen to share a value. If you ever see 0.046447
in output, check which one it is.

META-LESSON worth more than the result: "the discrepancy is the same in both
directions, so it must be X" felt like proof and was not. It eliminated one
explanation while leaving two standing. Checking whether the numbers were
EXACT rather than approximate is what surfaced the second one.


================================================================================
       16. SESSION 2 - THE MOVE-TO-POINT NODE
================================================================================

NEW  src/qarm_kinematics/qarm_kinematics/move_to_node.py
NEW  src/qarm_kinematics/launch/move_to.launch.py
     robot_params.py gains MAX_JOINT_SPEED (pi/2 rad/s, Table 7)

  ros2 launch qarm_kinematics move_to.launch.py rviz:=true
  ros2 topic pub --once /target_point geometry_msgs/msg/PointStamped \
      "{header: {frame_id: base_link}, point: {x: 0.3, y: 0.2, z: 0.5}}"

Subscribes geometry_msgs/PointStamped on target_point, solves IK from the arm's
CURRENT pose, walks every joint toward the answer at the rated speed, and
publishes sensor_msgs/JointState on joint_states so TF and RViz follow.
Parameters: rate (50 Hz), speed_scale (0.5), wrist ('N/A'), frame.

16.1  WHY A TOPIC AND A STANDARD MESSAGE, not a custom service

geometry_msgs/PointStamped already means "a point in a named frame", which is
exactly the input. A custom service would add a message package, a build
dependency and a bespoke type for NO NEW INFORMATION. A topic also lets a
future sequencer publish without blocking on a service call, and
`ros2 topic pub` is enough to drive it by hand.

The cost is that a topic gives no reply, so the outcome goes to the log and to
/move_to/done (std_msgs/Bool): true on arrival, false when the target is
refused. That is enough to chain moves later, and if a real request/response is
ever needed, the IK itself is a plain function call.

(Day 2's prompt asked for a service and said to propose the type first and
prefer a standard message. This is that proposal, with the reasoning.)

16.2  TWO PUBLISHERS, ONE TOPIC - and the remap it needs

qarm_move_to owns the four arm joints; qarm_gripper owns the two finger
joints; both publish to /joint_states. robot_state_publisher keeps the state
of every joint it has SEEN, updating only the names present in each message,
so two publishers owning disjoint joints works with no coordination between
them.

Consequences: no joint_state_publisher is needed at all in this launch file,
and nothing fights the IK for control of the arm joints - which the slider GUI
would, since it republishes every joint continuously.

**BUT IT NEEDS ONE REMAP, which the first version of the launch file was
missing.** qarm_gripper publishes to gripper/joint_states, NOT joint_states,
because display.launch.py feeds that topic into joint_state_publisher_gui's
source_list. move_to.launch.py has no joint_state_publisher, so nothing merged
it: the finger joints never reached robot_state_publisher and the finger
frames never appeared in TF at all. Found by the end-to-end probe, which
failed with

    tf2.LookupException: "finger_left" passed to lookupTransform argument
    source_frame does not exist.

Fixed with remappings=[('gripper/joint_states', 'joint_states')] on the
gripper node. The claim above is true; it just was not true of the code until
the probe said so. See challenge 20.

16.3  Behaviour details

  - Starts at the home pose and publishes continuously, so TF is valid
    immediately rather than only after the first command.
  - Targets stamped in a frame other than base_link are REFUSED with a
    warning, not silently assumed. (A point in camera coordinates sent to a
    base-frame solver would move the arm somewhere wrong and look like an IK
    bug.)
  - Logs the chosen family, elbow label, all four joint angles in degrees, the
    largest single-joint move and the estimated duration.
  - On arrival, logs the achieved position computed by FK, so the log alone
    shows whether it got there.

16.4  LIMITATION, documented in the module

It interpolates in JOINT space, so the gripper's path between two points is a
CURVE, not a straight line. Fine for "go there"; wrong for "draw a straight
edge". Straight Cartesian paths need trajectory generation (goal 6), which
would call IK repeatedly along an interpolated Cartesian path. It also knows
nothing about collisions.

       24. THINGS TO BE ABLE TO EXPLAIN IN AN INTERVIEW

Pulled out deliberately, because "an AI generated it" is not an acceptable
answer and these are the parts with real content:

  1. Why the elbow zero moved, and why the -beta now in DH row 3 is NOT the
     double-count bug that was fixed on Day 2. (Section 5.1.) The test that
     guards it and what the two failure modes would look like numerically
     (0.3465 and 0.350 versus the correct 0.353553).

  2. Why four IK candidates are only two arm shapes, and the one line of
     algebra that proves it: atan2(-r, h) = -atan2(r, h). (Section 12.) And
     why that means the backward family is half the workspace rather than a
     curiosity, given the elbow's -95 deg limit.

  3. Why "shortest move" is the smallest MAX |dq| and not the smallest sum.
     (Section 11.5.) It follows from all four joints sharing one speed limit.

  4. Why the round-trip test is the one that matters: it trusts no IK formula,
     only FK, which was independently validated against the URDF and the live
     TF tree. (Section 14.)

  5. The reach finding, and the fact that it corrected a piece of MY OWN
     earlier reasoning: "the discrepancy is identical in both directions, so it
     must be a tip extension" eliminated one explanation and left two
     standing. (Section 15.) This is the best example in the project of
     checking whether numbers are EXACT rather than merely close.

  6. Two numerical lessons of the same shape: sqrt(1 - x^2) near |x| = 1
     (section 6) and acos near 1 (section 13.1). In both cases the fix is to
     use the quantity you already have instead of passing it through a
     function that amplifies its error.

  7. Why the gripper node lives in qarm_description and the IK node in
     qarm_kinematics - a circular-dependency argument, not a preference.
     (Section 7.5.)

  8. Why two nodes can publish to /joint_states without coordinating.
     (Section 16.2.)

  9. Why colcon test alone cannot gate CI. (Section 9.) A small thing that
     invalidates an entire test suite in practice.

 10. The 4-DOF constraint: position uses up the first three joints, so the
     gripper's approach direction is not free. (Section 3.1.) This shapes any
     grasp design.
